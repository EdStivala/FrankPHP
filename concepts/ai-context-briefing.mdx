FrankPHP is built to be worked on with an AI assistant — Claude, ChatGPT or any other. The idea is simple: when you start a session, the assistant should understand FrankPHP and your app precisely, without guessing and without you explaining it all again.

That's the **AI Context Briefing**: two files, kept apart on purpose.

| File | Written by | Changes when | Describes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `framework/codebase.md` | FrankPHP | You update FrankPHP | How FrankPHP works: its patterns, rules and what it leaves out |
| `app/MYAPP.md` | You, and your AI | You add to your app | Your app: its tables, routes, services and rules |

---

## Why an AI needs a briefing

Without one, an AI assistant has two problems:

- **It can't tell good advice from bad.** It learned from everything it has read — official docs, old tutorials, forum workarounds — and it can't weigh one against another.
- **It doesn't know your app.** To learn your routes, models and rules, it has to read your files and guess at how they fit together.

The briefing fixes both: `codebase.md` gives it one accurate account of FrankPHP, and `MYAPP.md` gives it your app.

---

## `codebase.md` — how FrankPHP works

`codebase.md` describes everything an assistant needs to work with FrankPHP correctly: routing and middleware, the request cycle, controllers, models and services, views, the database, sign-in and tenants, and the CSS conventions.

It also says plainly what FrankPHP **leaves out** — no ORM, no template engine, no event system, no queues, no Composer. So an assistant reading it won't write code for features FrankPHP doesn't have.

<Warning>
`codebase.md` is replaced every time you update FrankPHP. Don't edit it, and don't let your AI edit it.
</Warning>

### Reuse Before You Add

`codebase.md` opens with a principle that applies to **your** code as much as FrankPHP's, in every layer. An assistant working from the briefing should:

- **reuse an existing method** before adding a new one, even if the call is shaped a little differently
- **write new methods as general operations,** never as one-off helpers
- **say which existing methods each change reuses,** and why any new method is needed
- **remove methods a change leaves unused,** and any that duplicate another

<Tip>
If your assistant proposes a new method without saying why an existing one won't do, ask it to check first.
</Tip>

## `MYAPP.md` — your app

`MYAPP.md` sits in `app/`, on your side of the [framework and app split](/concepts/revisions-and-migrations). You and your AI keep it up to date as your app grows. A FrankPHP update never touches it, and changing your app never touches `codebase.md`.

What goes in it, and how to keep it accurate, is in [Working with MYAPP.md](/getting-started/working-with-myapp).

---

## Using the briefing

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="With a chat assistant">
    Paste both files at the start of each session. The assistant then knows FrankPHP and your app, without you choosing files or explaining your conventions.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="With an AI agent">
    An agent that can read your files, such as Claude Code, should read both before it starts. They give it an accurate map, so it doesn't have to work out your conventions from the code.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Either way, the assistant is only as good as `MYAPP.md` is up to date.

---

## Why it pays off more over time

Early on, `MYAPP.md` is short, and the main benefit is that your assistant has an accurate account of FrankPHP.

As your app grows, the briefing matters more. With twenty routes, six models and a handful of services, a new AI session — or a new developer — can understand the whole app from two files. Handing a project over, or bringing someone in, gets much easier.
