Every FrankPHP app is multi-tenant from day one. This section explains how people get into your app, which tenant they belong to, and what their role lets them do.

---

## The three ideas you need

- **Tenant:** one customer account in your app — a business, a team or a personal workspace. Each tenant's data is kept apart, and every tenant page has the tenant's number in its address (`/tenant/{tenant_id}/...`).
- **User:** a person who signs in. Every user belongs to exactly one tenant, and an email address can belong to only one user.
- **Role:** what a user can do inside their tenant: `owner`, `user`, or `platform` for the people who run the site.

People sign in with a 6-digit code sent to their email, or with Google if you've turned it on. There are no passwords. Someone signing in with a new address gets their own new tenant, and becomes its owner.

---

## The roles

| Role | Who has it | What the starter app lets them do |
|------|------------|-----------------------------------|
| `owner` | The person a tenant was created for | Tenant Settings, the User Screen, invite people to join their tenant, edit or remove its users, delete the tenant |
| `user` | Everyone else in the tenant, usually invited by the owner | Their own User Settings, and deleting their own account |
| `platform` | You, and anyone else who runs the site | The User Screen for every tenant, edit or delete any tenant, invite someone to a brand-new tenant, and sign in as a tenant's owner to help them |

<Note>
The framework supplies the operations, such as deleting a user or changing a role. **Who may use them, and on which screen, is decided by your app.** The table shows the starter app's rules, which you can change in your own `app/`.
</Note>

## Not available yet

<Warning>
These are not part of FrankPHP 3.0. Don't build on them yet.

- **Microsoft sign-in.** Its settings exist in `app/env.example`, but they turn nothing on. It's planned for a future release.
- **A screen for changing roles.** The framework can change a user's role, but the starter app has no screen for it.
- **Re-accepting terms** when your terms version changes, and **a consent history** of every acceptance.
- **Finer-grained privileges,** such as per-feature permissions or limits tied to a subscription.
</Warning>
